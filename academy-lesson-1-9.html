Maintenance versus change control

Like-for-like replacement can follow a controlled maintenance route if approved procedures and a documented assessment support that classification. GMP-significant impacts on qualified status, alarm limits, monitoring, calibration or product quality may instead require formal change control. In either case, document the work, verify suitability and obtain required approvals before returning the system to GMP use.

Readable guide to the video diagrams

Text descriptions of the main slide relationships, in presentation order. Use these with the narration transcript and companion reading. Arrows summarize relationships; they do not replace site procedures or prescribe a universal workflow. The repeated right-hand panels are reminders, not additional mandatory steps.

  1. Change control connects proposal, assessment and closure; evaluate change before affecting GMP operations.
  2. Change request compares the current state, proposed state and rationale, identifying affected systems.
  3. Impact assessment covers product quality, validation status and regulatory status.
  4. Risk-based verification considers possible failure, controls and verification.
  5. Approval gates: assessment and authorization precede execution.
  6. Implementation: execute, verify and document evidence and deviations; complete needed qualification and updates.
  7. Closure considers records, training, monitoring and residual risk.
  8. HVAC sensor example: request, assess, calibrate and verify that replacement maintains the qualified function.
