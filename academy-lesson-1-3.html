Supplementary reading: Personnel, hygiene and gowning

Training and qualification: Personnel must receive training appropriate to assigned duties, including GMP principles and job-specific procedures. Training attendance alone does not demonstrate practical competence; supervisors should assess gowning technique and task performance where relevant.

Practical example: If an operator discovers a missed required gowning step before entering a controlled area, stop and follow the approved procedure. Report the event and assess whether the area or product could have been affected. Do not assume a clean appearance proves the absence of contamination.

Regulatory context: US 21 CFR 211.25 addresses personnel qualifications and training; 211.28 addresses clothing, hygiene and sanitation. EU GMP Volume 4 Chapter 2 covers personnel and training; EU GMP Annex 1 provides additional controls for sterile manufacturing. Requirements depend on the activity and applicable market.

Readable guide to the video diagrams

Text descriptions of the main slide relationships, in presentation order. Use these with the narration transcript and companion reading. Arrows summarize relationships; they do not replace site procedures or prescribe a universal workflow. The repeated right-hand panels are reminders, not additional mandatory steps.

  1. Personnel responsibilities: understand, perform and report.
  2. Training: train, demonstrate competence and reassess; attendance alone is insufficient.
  3. Hygiene: hand hygiene, appropriate clothing and reporting relevant conditions.
  4. Gowning: prepare, gown and verify according to the area-specific procedure.
  5. Controlled-area behaviour: follow flows, minimize risk and escalate concerns.
  6. Contamination prevention: identify, segregate and verify materials and status.
  7. Supervision: supervise, document and review actual work.
  8. Missed gowning step: stop, report and assess under the approved procedure.
