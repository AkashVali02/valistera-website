Qualification lifecycle and cleaning validation — supplementary reading

Qualification commonly progresses from documented user requirements and design qualification (DQ) through installation qualification (IQ), operational qualification (OQ), and performance qualification (PQ), where applicable and justified. Requalification or periodic evaluation helps establish that equipment, facilities and utilities remain suitable for intended use; frequency and scope depend on risk, system history and applicable requirements.

For EU GMP medicinal-product operations, Annex 15 Section 10 addresses cleaning validation of product-contact equipment; a visual check alone is not generally sufficient. Establish scientifically justified cleaning procedures, limits, sampling and verification according to the equipment, product and contamination risk. This is distinct from routine cleaning execution records.

European Commission: EU GMP Annex 15

Readable guide to the video diagrams

Text descriptions of the main slide relationships, in presentation order. Use these with the narration transcript and companion reading. Arrows summarize relationships; they do not replace site procedures or prescribe a universal workflow. The repeated right-hand panels are reminders, not additional mandatory steps.

  1. Premises: design, maintain and monitor for the intended process.
  2. Material/personnel flow: receive, control movement and produce while preventing contamination and mix-ups.
  3. Cleaning: follow the procedure, clean and verify effectiveness.
  4. Equipment design connects suitable materials, cleanability and intended use.
  5. Calibration and maintenance: calibrate, maintain and record; assess overdue work.
  6. Qualification: IQ, OQ and PQ mean installation, operational and performance qualification. Scope and acceptance criteria are system-specific.
  7. Status: identify equipment, check status and record; labels complement controlled records.
  8. Expired calibration: hold, report and assess. The revised scenario concerns a measuring instrument used with a vessel, not calibration of the vessel itself.
