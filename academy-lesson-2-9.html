Part 1 of 4

A deliberately small dataset

Key explanation · Part 1

The learning dashboard contains ten fictional temperature-indication errors in degrees Celsius: 0.1, 0.2, 0.1, 0.3, 0.2, 0.4, 0.3, 0.5, 0.4 and 0.6. They illustrate a time-ordered pattern. They are not calibration data from a real instrument and do not define a pharmaceutical tolerance.

Part 2 of 4

Calculate transparently

Key explanation · Part 2

The total is 3.1 °C across ten errors, giving a mean error of 0.31 °C. The range is 0.6 minus 0.1, or 0.5 °C. The highest error is 0.6 °C. The chart includes the underlying values in a table so that learners can check the arithmetic without relying on colour or vision. These summaries describe this small dataset only.

Part 3 of 4

Pass limits are not trend limits

Key explanation · Part 3

For arithmetic practice only, set an illustrative absolute-error limit of 0.5 °C. Nine of the ten observations meet that example limit and one does not. The limit is not an official criterion and does not qualify an instrument. The upward pattern also deserves attention even before a limit is exceeded. In a real investigation, examine measurement uncertainty, reference suitability, conditions and data integrity before drawing conclusions.

Part 4 of 4

Do not infer capability

Key explanation · Part 4

Do not calculate a confident capability claim from these ten convenient values. A meaningful analysis requires a suitable study design and understanding of the measurement system and process behaviour. In the activity, change the illustrative limit, observe the count and explain what information is still missing. More green points do not create more knowledge. The purpose is to practice transparent calculation and proportionate interpretation, not to approve a system.

Required reading: signed and absolute error

Each listed value is signed error: indication minus reference, in °C. All ten happen to be positive. The decision compares |error| with the illustrative limit; equality passes this numerical comparison. A −0.6 °C error would also exceed a 0.5 °C absolute limit.

The signed mean is +0.31 °C; range is 0.50 °C. Four absolute errors exceed 0.30 °C; one exceeds 0.50 °C. Measurement uncertainty and an approved decision rule are still needed for real conformity decisions. Ten observations do not establish capability or stability.
