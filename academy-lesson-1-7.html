OOS investigation — supplementary reading

An out-of-specification (OOS) result is not erased or replaced by a passing retest. Preserve the original data, promptly notify the appropriate quality function and follow the approved laboratory OOS procedure. A timely initial laboratory assessment examines possible analytical errors and available evidence. If no scientifically supported laboratory cause explains the result, a broader manufacturing and quality investigation may be necessary.

Invalidating an initial result requires a documented, scientifically justified assignable cause established through investigation. Retesting and resampling must be controlled and scientifically justified; repeating a test until a passing result appears is not acceptable. Out-of-trend (OOT) results may also warrant investigation under the site's trending and quality procedures even when within specification.

Reference for further reading: FDA Guidance for Industry, Investigating Out-of-Specification (OOS) Test Results for Pharmaceutical Production (May 2022), and applicable GMP requirements. This summary is educational and does not prescribe a universal investigation sequence for every product and market.
