GMP Manufacturing Operations and In-Process Controls

Lesson 1.5 · Narrated learning · Optional captions and key explanations

Technical reference for the revised recording: Independent dispensing checks are not optional merely because a local risk assessment prefers otherwise. EU GMP Chapter 5, 5.37–5.38 addresses controlled dispensing and a recorded independent check of each dispensed material and its weight or volume. US 21 CFR 211.101(c)–(d) sets second-person checks with defined automated-equipment exceptions under 211.68.

Dispensing and verification — jurisdiction-specific requirements

For US finished pharmaceutical manufacturing, 21 CFR 211.101(c) requires a second-person examination of each dispensed component container for release status, correct weight or measure, and proper identification, with a specified exception for weighing, measuring or subdividing performed by automated equipment under 21 CFR 211.68. Under 21 CFR 211.101(d), component addition requires second-person verification for manual addition; a defined single-person verification applies when the operation is performed by qualifying automated equipment.

Other jurisdictions and product categories may differ. Follow applicable regulations and approved procedures rather than treating every independent check as optional based only on site risk.

Readable guide to the video diagrams

Text descriptions of the main slide relationships, in presentation order. Use these with the narration transcript and companion reading. Arrows summarize relationships; they do not replace site procedures or prescribe a universal workflow. The repeated right-hand panels are reminders, not additional mandatory steps.

  1. Manufacturing control: approved instructions, execution and review.
  2. Material status has distinct quarantine, released and rejected states. Verify records, not only container labels.
  3. Dispensing connects material identity, measurement and recording. Perform applicable independent checks and maintain batch traceability.
  4. Line clearance: clear the area, check setup and authorize the operation under approved procedures.
  5. In-process control loop: measure, compare with approved limits, then respond and record.
  6. Batch records connect operations, in-process data and reconciliation; investigate unexplained gaps.
  7. Deviation response: secure, report and assess while preserving evidence.
  8. Conflicting status: a released label and a system hold are inconsistent. Hold the material and escalate; do not select the preferred status.
