From installed to challenged

Lesson explanation

Welcome to Module 5, Operational Qualification, or OQ. We continue the fictional V-201 oral-liquid vessel. Installation evidence identifies the system. An operational test asks what that system actually does under a defined condition. All records and values here are invented teaching examples, not approved site instructions.

Part 2 of 4

Keep evidence boundaries clear

View Technical Explanation · Diagram 2

Lesson explanation

A sensor serial number supports identity. A controlled input and measured response support a functional conclusion. Product mixing performance needs its own justified study. These evidence questions can be coordinated, but a successful operational test alone does not establish blend uniformity, cleaning effectiveness or permission to manufacture.

Part 3 of 4

Challenge the intended range

View Technical Explanation · Diagram 3

Lesson explanation

A comfortable set point is only one part of operation. Select meaningful operating conditions from the intended use and system knowledge. A minimum level may challenge one function while a higher load challenges another. Explain why the selected conditions expose relevant failure mechanisms, rather than treating a maximum value as universally worst case.

Part 4 of 4

Bound the conclusion

View Technical Explanation · Diagram 4

Lesson explanation

The conclusion identifies the tested configuration, conditions and results. It also identifies what was not demonstrated. A simulation that checks the controller may leave a physical sensor or actuator untested. This is a useful partial result when its boundary is explicit, not evidence for an unchallenged complete loop.
