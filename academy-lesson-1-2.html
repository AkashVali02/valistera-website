Practical GDP example — supplementary reading

If a GMP record contains an error, follow the approved site procedure. For a paper entry, a typical controlled correction preserves the original entry legibly (for example, one line through the incorrect value), identifies the correction, and records the responsible person's initials or signature, date and reason where required. Never obscure or erase the original entry, use correction fluid, or invent an activity time.

If an entry was missed, make a clearly identified late entry using the actual entry date/time and an explanation consistent with the applicable procedure; do not backdate. Independent verification or second-person checks apply where required by regulation, approved procedure or risk controls, not automatically to every GDP correction.

Example: An operator discovers that a process step's time was omitted. Preserve the record, promptly inform the responsible supervisor or QA as required, establish what reliable contemporaneous evidence exists, and document a traceable correction or deviation under the approved procedure. Never estimate a time and present it as observed fact.
