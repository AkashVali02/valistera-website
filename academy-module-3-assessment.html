Apply the DQ reasoning

20 distinct application questions. This open-book self-study assessment exposes its answer key in the source and below. The suggested practice target is 80%, not a regulatory threshold, DQ acceptance rule or proof of independent competence. Unanswered questions must be completed before scoring. Answers are not sent to a server.

1. A team signs DQ after reviewing drawings. Which statement can its conclusion support?

2. The URS has revision 3 but the DQ matrix cites revision 2. What is needed?

3. A vendor guarantees a catalogue maximum volume; the process minimum is undefined. What remains unresolved?

4. A fictional indication requirement is ±0.5 °C. Three applicable component bounds are ±0.2 °C each. Which conservative total applies?

5. The vendor proposes ±0.1, ±0.1 and ±0.2 °C components. Against the fictional ±0.5 °C limit, what follows?

6. A gasket has a known material name but no product/cleaning compatibility basis. What is justified?

7. An outlet drawing is revised to remove a retained pocket. Which closure is strongest?

8. A control loop and its alarm use a common transmitter. What should the DQ review examine?

9. A fault response inhibits heating, but restart behaviour is unspecified. What action fits DQ?

10. A password is shown on the control screen. What does it demonstrate by itself?

11. A rear instrument cannot be removed after piping is added. Which input was missed?

12. A utility is qualified for its own scope. What else supports a vessel connection?

13. An action owner is assigned for a protective-control gap. How should evidence status change?

14. A low risk total hides a severe effect with uncertain detection. Which response is strongest?

15. The supplier protocol omits an identified sensor-failure challenge. Which action is appropriate?

16. A FAT result is proposed for reuse after a design change. Which question matters most?

17. A required gasket specification is changed during DQ. What preserves traceability?

18. A proposed next activity is unrelated to an open design gap. What supports progression?

19. Eight design rows are supported and two remain open. What is the numerical coverage?

20. What should accompany a DQ package handed to the IQ team?

Answer key and explanations

1. Proposed design suitability was evaluated within the stated scope — Limit the conclusion to the evidence and scope of the design review.

2. Assess changed requirements and reconcile the review baseline — A revision change can alter the design-review basis.

3. Suitability across the intended operating volume range — Supplier capability cannot define the missing process need.

4. ±0.6 °C — The aligned worst-case sum is 0.2 + 0.2 + 0.2 = 0.6 °C, not a full uncertainty budget.

5. The 0.4 °C sum supports further design review, subject to applicability — The conservative sum is within the teaching limit, but conditions and later verification remain relevant.

6. Request evidence relevant to the intended exposure conditions — Identity is necessary evidence but does not alone prove suitability.

7. Reference the reviewed revision and retain installed/cleaning verification — The closure needs reviewed evidence and must retain future verification obligations.

8. Whether shared failures undermine the proposed protection — Evaluate the failure mechanism rather than counting devices or labels.

9. Request justified recovery behaviour and link it to verification — Recovery can affect risk and must be addressed as relevant to intended use.

10. Only the presence of an access feature, not adequate system control — A login feature does not establish full access, record or computerized-system compliance.

11. Maintenance access in the installed arrangement — Review access with the relevant installed configuration and dimensions.

12. A comparison of supply conditions with vessel design needs — System qualification does not automatically establish interface compatibility.

13. Remain unresolved until suitable evidence is reviewed — Ownership and schedule are management information, not evidence of effectiveness.

14. Examine severity, uncertainty and the basis of the proposed control — A score alone cannot settle an unsupported high-consequence scenario.

15. Arrange site suitability review and a justified verification supplement — The site must assess protocol suitability and coverage of relevant verification needs.

16. Is its configuration/function still applicable after the change? — Evidence reuse depends on applicable configuration, conditions and impacts, not presentation.

17. Retain the prior baseline and assess affected conclusions — Controlled history and impact review allow the decision to be reconstructed.

18. A documented impact assessment and authorized stage decision — Progression is specific to the proposed activity and does not close the design finding.

19. 80% — 8/10 × 100 = 80%; this does not establish suitability or a release threshold.

20. Controlled design baseline, findings and verification dependencies — IQ needs the accepted design basis and visibility of unresolved dependencies.
