Printable answer key and explanations

1. A defined intended use with product context — Intended use provides the basis for requirements and evidence; a brochure or signature alone does not.

2. The executed record and evaluated report — The plan and quotation describe intentions; executed records and the report provide evidence and evaluation.

3. Yes, through the process it controls — An indirect control pathway can affect quality even without product contact.

4. Document and assess the failure through controlled procedures — A failed result needs documented evaluation; a requirement change must be justified and controlled.

5. No, results and deviations still need evaluation — Execution and acceptance are distinct; evidence must meet criteria and receive the appropriate evaluation.

6. Defining the failure scenario and evidence — A meaningful score depends on a well-defined scenario and an evidence-based rationale.

7. Not necessarily; component scores and context matter — Equal products can conceal different severity, uncertainty and control needs.

8. After appropriate effectiveness evidence and evaluation — The control must address the failure and work in context; purchase or proposal alone is insufficient.

9. A descriptive summary for the exercise — They support the stated arithmetic exercise, not qualification, capability or regulatory acceptance.

10. Readiness is not demonstrated; evaluate and resolve the gaps — Completion percentage cannot replace evaluation of important unresolved evidence.

11. Design review pending, with owner and document reference — A specific status and evidence reference expose remaining work.

12. An interface owner and relevant utility evidence — The boundary must not create a gap in responsibility or evidence.

13. A temperature tolerance awaiting process justification — Missing process justification must be resolved; the statement is not ready for acceptance testing.

14. No; assess applicability and integrity — Configuration and transport/installation effects can change applicability.

15. Harder detection before the consequence — This teaching scale uses higher values for greater detection difficulty; scales must be defined.

16. A possible common-cause failure — A shared dependency may undermine claimed independence.

17. 0.31 °C — Their sum is 3.1 °C; divided by ten gives 0.31 °C.

18. One — Only 0.6 °C exceeds the illustrative limit; 0.5 equals it.

19. Assess scope, requirements, risks and affected evidence — The new intended use may affect several linked documents and controls.

20. Self-study completion only — Formal competency and authorization require the applicable independent/site process.
