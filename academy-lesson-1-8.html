Supplementary reading: Deviations, investigations and CAPA

Deviation: Record departures from approved processes or expected conditions promptly, contain any immediate risk, preserve evidence and assess potential impact on product quality. Investigation depth should be proportionate to risk and follow approved procedures.

CAPA: Corrective action addresses identified causes of an existing issue; preventive action seeks to prevent potential problems. Root-cause analysis, action ownership, deadlines and effectiveness verification should be appropriate to the investigation. Not every event necessarily requires a separate CAPA, but its disposition must be justified and documented.

Practical example: After a differential-pressure excursion, preserve monitoring trends and alarm records, document the affected time and activities, assess environmental and product impact, and determine whether corrective actions or further investigation are needed.

Regulatory context: US 21 CFR 211.192 addresses review and investigation of unexplained discrepancies and specification failures. EU GMP Volume 4 Chapter 1 addresses the pharmaceutical quality system, including deviation management and CAPA principles. ICH Q9(R1) provides quality-risk-management guidance, not an exemption from GMP obligations.
