Activity 1 · What operational qualification establishes

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Classify a serial-number check, a simulated high-temperature response and a blend-uniformity study. State one limitation of each.

Model reasoning

Identity belongs to IQ; a defined response is OQ evidence within its tested boundary; blend uniformity needs performance/process evidence. A simulated signal may exclude the sensor and physical output.

Activity 2 · Establish safe and controlled OQ readiness

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Draft a readiness decision for a display review and a wetted heating challenge while gasket traceability remains open.

Model reasoning

Keep the material gap visible. Hold dependent wetted work. A display review requires a defined boundary, documented no-significant-impact assessment and responsible authorization; do not infer permission from the course example.

Activity 3 · Design traceable tests and acceptance criteria

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Write a test row for a four-second teaching response limit, including start, endpoint, clock basis and evidence.

Model reasoning

Identify the function, approved criterion, controlled input, endpoint, timestamp resolution/alignment, actual elapsed time, raw record and reviewer. The four-second value is fictional and must not be reused as a site limit.

Activity 4 · Select operating limits and worst-case conditions

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Propose a test-condition matrix for two loads and three speeds, explaining one possible worst-case interaction and its limitations.

Model reasoning

The full teaching matrix has six pairs. Identify the mechanism being stressed and the approved safe range. Do not claim every combination must always be run or that maximum speed is automatically worst case.

Activity 5 · Challenge alarms, interlocks and protective responses

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Write separate observations for an alarm and heater inhibition. Explain the scope of a simulated-input test.

Model reasoning

Identify input, alarm annunciation, actual output response, timing, reset and evidence. State excluded sensor/actuator elements and any complementary test. Do not infer heater isolation from display text.

Activity 6 · Evaluate control response and measurement confidence

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Compare simple-limit and stated guard-band decisions for error +0.4 °C, expanded uncertainty 0.2 °C and limit 0.5 °C.

Model reasoning

The simple absolute-error comparison is within 0.5 °C. Under the expressly stated illustrative rule, 0.4 + 0.2 = 0.6 °C and is not accepted. Neither calculation proves general suitability; define the uncertainty basis and applicable rule.

Activity 7 · Test interruptions and recovery within defined boundaries

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Draft a recovery review for a fictional interrupted test: configuration, records, output state and restart authority.

Model reasoning

Identify interruption boundary, approved method, expected state, settings, data and timestamps, recovery observations and restart decision. Do not give live fault-injection instructions or claim full CSV completion.

Activity 8 · Execute tests and preserve defensible evidence

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Describe how to handle a missing trend interval and where the partial original and repeat record will be retained.

Model reasoning

Preserve the partial original, record the gap, investigate its impact, authorize the justified repeat or continuation and cross-reference all data. Never reconstruct missing measurements from memory.

Revisit lesson

Activity 9 · Investigate failures and control retesting

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Outline an investigation for an alarm output that failed once and passed after a parameter change.

Model reasoning

Retain both results, identify the parameter/version change, establish cause with evidence, assess related functions and earlier tests, authorize retest scope and review closure. A single pass does not erase the original failure.

Revisit lesson

Activity 10 · Report OQ and prepare the next-stage decision

Suggested time: 20 minutes. Revisit the narrated lesson and worked example before responding.

Prepare an OQ handover when eleven checks pass and one protective test fails. State the evidence, restriction and next-stage decision.

Model reasoning

Retain the failed protective function, identify impact and restrict dependent activities. Reconcile evidence and configuration, specify closure needs and request responsible review. Approximately 91.7% is a descriptive pass rate, not acceptance.

Capstone rubric

Give each dimension 0 (missing), 1 (partial) or 2 (supported): controlled baseline; criteria and actual observations; evidence traceability; discrepancy impact and verification; bounded handover recommendation. This 10-point self-review rubric is not a competency certification. Invented evidence or unsupported routine-use approval requires correction irrespective of the score.
